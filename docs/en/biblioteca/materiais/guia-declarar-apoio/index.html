Guide: how to disclose support and advertising in content

What to say, where to place it and when a support notice is mandatory, with the most common mistakes in videos, stories and text.

When the notice is mandatory

Any piece made in exchange for payment, barter, a received product or a paid trip has to say so to the audience. The rule applies to public interest campaigns the same way it applies to consumer products: support from a foundation or an institute is support, and it gets disclosed.

It does not matter whether the supporter asked for the notice or whether the creator agrees with the cause. The criterion is where the money came from, not the opinion of the person publishing.

Content made on the creator's own initiative about a topic already covered in a paid campaign does not need a notice, as long as there is no payment and no request from the supporter. When in doubt, the notice costs less than the suspicion.

How to write the notice

Use wording anyone understands on first read: 'advertising', 'paid content', 'supported by' followed by the organization's name. Acronyms, hashtags buried at the end of the caption and terms like 'partnership' or 'collab' do not do the job.

The notice goes at the start, not the end. In video, it appears on screen and is said out loud in the first seconds. In stories, it goes on every card in the sequence, because people may start watching from the middle. In text, it comes before the first paragraph.

When the platform has its own paid content marker, the marker adds to the written notice and does not replace it. The marker disappears in reposts and screenshots; the text stays.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

Disclosing only in the first video of a series. Each piece is watched on its own and each piece carries the notice.

Naming the supporter vaguely, as 'a partner organization'. The full name lets the audience look up who paid and weigh the interest behind the message.

Treating the notice as an embarrassment. In the network's tracking, a clear notice did not reduce the reach of the pieces, and it is the criterion supporters use most when deciding to hire the same creator again.
