Briefing for organizations: hiring creators without interfering in editing

What to write in the brief, what to leave out and how to approve a script without turning the creator into a spokesperson.

What goes in the brief

The brief says what the organization needs the audience to know or do, and for whom. One core message, two at most, each written in a single sentence. If the organization cannot sum it up that way, the problem is the objective, not the creator.

Say what cannot be said: figures the organization does not stand behind, promises it cannot keep, comparisons that create legal risk. That list is the real boundary of the campaign and saves review rounds.

Include the facts and sources the creator may use, with links. The creator should not have to research what the organization already knows.

Delivery deadline, format, number of pieces and fee per piece belong in the contract, not the brief. Mixing the two makes the creator read the brief as a work order.

What stays out

A finished script, text to read, a shot order. The creator was hired because the audience trusts the way they speak. If the organization writes the lines, it buys the reach and loses the trust.

Adjectives about the organization. The audience does not need to know that the foundation is 'a reference' or 'a pioneer'; it needs to understand the subject.

Exclusivity beyond the campaign period and silence clauses on related topics. Both drive away the most respected creators and do not change the result of the piece.

How to approve without editing

Approval checks three things: the core message is there, nothing from the list of prohibitions was said and the support is disclosed. Beyond that it is opinion, and opinion is not grounds for rejection.

One review round on the script and one on the final video. Each round has a response deadline for the organization, usually two business days. A missed deadline counts as approval.

Comments go in a list, with the passage quoted and the reason tied to one of the three criteria. 'I did not like the tone' is not a comment; 'the figure at minute two contradicts the source we sent' is.
