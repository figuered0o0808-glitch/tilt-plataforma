Report: how to measure public interest campaigns

Which indicators show whether a campaign changed what people know or do, and why reach and likes do not answer that question.

What reach measures and what it does not

Reach counts how many screens displayed the piece. It does not say whether anyone watched to the end, understood it or did anything different afterwards. For a campaign selling a product that may be enough; for one trying to change a habit or warn about a risk, it is not.

Likes and comments measure reaction to the piece, not to the topic. The report compares twelve public interest campaigns the observatory tracked in 2025 and 2026: the correlation between engagement and change measured in a follow-up survey was weak in ten of them.

Retention and watch time are the platform indicators closest to comprehension. A three-minute piece watched to the end by 40% of the audience informed more than a thirty-second one with three times the reach.

Indicators that answer the question

Define before the campaign what needs to change: a fact the audience did not have, an action it was not taking. The indicator comes out of that definition. Without it, any number will do and none will decide.

Measure the baseline before the first piece goes live, with a short survey of the creator's own audience: three questions in a poll or form. Repeat the same survey two weeks after the last piece. The difference is the campaign's result.

When the campaign asks for an action that leaves a trace, such as signing up, consulting a service or using a link, measure the action with a separate link per creator. Cost per action is the indicator that helps most in deciding the next campaign.

Use a comparison group whenever possible: a city, an age group or an audience that did not receive the campaign. Without comparison, a rise in searches for the topic may be the news cycle, not the campaign.

How to present the result

A campaign report is one page: objective, baseline, result, cost per result and what would be done differently. Reach and engagement go in an appendix.

Break the result down by creator and by format. In the campaigns tracked, the gap between the most and the least efficient creator, measured in cost per action, was sixfold within the same campaign.

Record what did not work with the same precision as what did. An organization that only reports success cannot justify a change of strategy in the next campaign.
