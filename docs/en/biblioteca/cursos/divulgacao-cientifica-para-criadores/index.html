Science communication for content creators

How to go from a published study to a video or post that survives fact-checking, without inflating the result or hiding the uncertainty.

Class open

Most people who make content about science start from a headline, not from the study. The course reverses that order: each module opens with a real, published paper and ends with a piece ready for the platform the participant chooses.

Live classes run twice a week, in the evening, and are recorded. Between sessions, the asynchronous material covers reading tables, finding primary sources and exercises graded by the LabDados team.

The course is offered by INDICA in partnership with LabDados. By the end, each participant has published one piece of content and kept a record of how they measured whether the message reached the audience.

Who it is for

  • Creators who publish about health, climate or technology and have already been challenged on a source.
  • Journalists and communicators moving to short formats who need to keep the rigor.
  • Researchers who want to speak to a lay audience without depending on their institution's press office.

Course program

6 modules
  1. 01

    Reading a study

    Where to look first in a scientific paper: design, sample, main result and what the authors admit they do not know.

  2. 02

    Uncertainty and consensus

    How to say a result is preliminary without sounding evasive, and how to tell an isolated study from a field consensus.

  3. 03

    Sources and fact-checking

    Getting to the primary source from a news story, and checking authorship, funding and peer review before citing.

  4. 04

    Format by platform

    The same finding as a short video, a carousel and a long text: what fits in each and what gets lost.

  5. 05

    Errors and public correction

    What to do when published content is wrong: correct it, notify those who shared it and keep a record of the correction.

  6. 06

    Measuring whether the message landed

    Comments, retention and audience questions as indicators of understanding, not just reach.
