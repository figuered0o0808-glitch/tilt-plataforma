Grounds for the decision

The panel received 61 applications and assessed each one against the call's four criteria, in order of weight. Twenty-three proposals passed to the second stage for demonstrating ties to the territory; of those, nine went to interview. The final decision favored projects with direct access to the people portrayed and a publication plan already in place, even when the creator's audience was small. Two projects received less than requested, after production items were adjusted and absorbed by Koko. The final distribution supports four projects in four states, in distinct formats, so that the campaign reaches different audiences with the same funds. Proposals not selected received individual written feedback.

Overview

The electricity bill weighs more where income is lower. In peripheral neighborhoods, irregular connections, a social tariff few people know about and rooftops without solar panels are the rule, and the public debate on energy transition rarely passes through there.

The call supported creators who live or produce in those territories to talk about energy from the experience of the person who pays the bill: what it costs, what rights exist and what can be generated at home or in the neighborhood.

Instituto Corrente Limpa funded the call. INDICA ran the selection and the contract with each creator, and Koko shared production. It was the program's pilot call, and its result shaped the format of the calls that followed.

What is supported

  • Original projects about the cost, access or generation of energy in peripheral neighborhoods of any Brazilian city.
  • Videos, post series, podcasts or live streams, with a defined publication plan.
  • Creators as individuals or companies who live or produce in the territory portrayed.
  • Itemized budget between R$ 5,000 and R$ 15,000.

What is not supported

  • Advertising content for energy distributors or equipment manufacturers.
  • Projects with no link to a specific territory.

Selection criteria

The criteria are listed in order of weight.

1.

Link to the territory

The creator's ties to the neighborhood portrayed and access to the people the content is about.

2.

Usefulness of the content

How much the audience learns about the cost, rights or generation of energy from the project.

3.

Expected reach relative to the creator's scale

The reach expected given the audience size of whoever proposes, not the size itself.

4.

Feasibility

Coherence between scope, budget and schedule.
