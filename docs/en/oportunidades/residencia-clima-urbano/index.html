Overview

Extreme heat is the climate event that kills the most people in cities, and it kills unequally. Someone who lives on a street without trees, works outdoors or sleeps under an uninsulated concrete roof feels several degrees more than the weather station records. The news treats a heat wave as a temperature record; the residency wants it treated as a matter of housing, work and health.

Six creators spend eight weeks inside the observatory, with access to surface temperature maps of twelve state capitals, to the research team and to a recording studio. Two weeks are in person at the observatory's office; the other six are remote, with weekly mentoring. Each creator arrives with a project and leaves with a series published on their own channel and distributed through the observatory's networks.

There is no fixed support amount. The stipend is agreed with each selected creator according to city of origin, time commitment and the project's production cost, and is formalized before the start. Observatório Clima Urbano runs the residency; INDICA handles applications, contracting and payment.

What is supported

  • Projects on extreme heat in Brazilian cities: who suffers most, why, and what the city can do.
  • Individual creators with an active channel on any platform and availability for eight weeks, two of them in person.
  • Use of the observatory's data with source attribution and technical review before publication.
  • A series of at least three pieces published on the creator's channel by the end of the residency.
  • Written proposals or video proposals of up to three minutes, with a portfolio of up to five works.

What is not supported

  • One-off coverage of heat waves without a series project.
  • Content with commercial sponsorship from a product related to the theme, such as air conditioners or beverages.

Selection criteria

The criteria are listed in order of weight.

1.

Angle on heat inequality

The project starts from the people who suffer most from heat and from what changes for them, not from the temperature record.

2.

Use of data

Ability to turn temperature measurements and heat maps into a narrative the audience understands and can verify.

3.

Relationship with the audience

Coherence between the public the creator already reaches and the public the residency wants to reach: residents of outlying neighborhoods, outdoor workers and local officials.
