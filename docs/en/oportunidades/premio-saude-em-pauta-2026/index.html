Overview

The Saúde em Pauta Award recognizes public health content published by creators in 2026. No project is submitted: entrants submit a piece already published, with its public address and publication date. The panel judges what the audience saw, not what was intended.

Rede Saúde em Pauta rewards published content because the problem it faces is the gap between what science knows and what circulates on social networks. A piece that has already been through that contest, with reach figures and comments open to verification, shows more about the creator than a proposal does.

There are three categories, each with a R$ 20,000 award: prevention, mental health and vaccination. Each creator could enter up to two pieces, in different categories. A category with no piece approved by the panel goes without an award; the money does not move to another category. Entries are closed and the result is published on 10 November 2026.

What is supported

  • Pieces published between 1 January and 30 September 2026, on any network or website, with a public address that allows date and reach to be checked.
  • Prevention: habits, screening and continued care that avoid illness or harm, in line with public health system guidance.
  • Mental health: psychological distress, access to care and stigma reduction, without prescribing treatment.
  • Vaccination: the immunization schedule, coverage and answers to frequent doubts, with the source identified in the piece.
  • Video, audio, text, carousel or short series, in Portuguese, published on the entrant's own channel.
  • Up to two pieces per creator, in different categories.

What is not supported

  • A piece published before 2026 or edited after the entry deadline.
  • Content paid for by a manufacturer of medicines, vaccines or supplements, or by a health insurer, even when labeled as advertising.
  • A piece that recommends treatment, dosage or replacing medical advice.
  • Institutional content from a news outlet, public body or company.

Selection criteria

The criteria are listed in order of weight.

1.

Accuracy

Correctness of the information, with a verifiable source that was current on the publication date.

2.

Clarity for people outside the field

How far the piece makes a health decision understandable to someone without training in the area, without simplifying to the point of error.

3.

Response relative to the audience

Reach and conversation generated in proportion to the channel's size, measured by the piece's public figures and its comments.
